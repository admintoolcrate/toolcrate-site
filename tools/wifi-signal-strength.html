How to read this

WiFi signal strength is measured in dBm (decibel-milliwatts), a logarithmic scale where values are negative and closer to zero means stronger — −30 dBm is an excellent signal standing next to the router, while −90 dBm is barely usable. Browsers have no API to read your actual WiFi signal strength directly, so this tool interprets a number you look up yourself, typically in your OS's network settings or a router admin page.

Rough guide: −50 dBm or better is excellent (full bars, fast and reliable); −50 to −60 is good for browsing and video calls; −60 to −70 is fair — usable but may stutter on video or large downloads; below −70 is poor and often means dropped connections or very slow speeds. These bands are commonly used guidelines, not a universal standard — different devices and router vendors draw the lines slightly differently.

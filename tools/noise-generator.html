About the different noise colors

White noise has equal energy at every frequency — it sounds like TV static or a hiss, and is good at masking sudden, varied sounds. Pink noise reduces higher frequencies (power falls off at 3 dB per octave), sounding softer and closer to steady rain or wind. Brown noise (technically "red noise") falls off even faster (6 dB per octave), producing a deep, low rumble like a waterfall or distant thunder — often preferred for sleep since it has less high-frequency hiss.

Sound is generated live with the Web Audio API from random values, not a looped audio file, so it never audibly repeats. It keeps playing as long as this tab stays open, even if you switch to another tab — closing the tab or navigating away stops it. Nothing is recorded or uploaded.
